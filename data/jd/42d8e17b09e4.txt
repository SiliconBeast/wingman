Your Role

We’re looking for Product Management interns to join us at Hudl’s Headquarters in Lincoln, Nebraska. As a Product Management intern, you’ll think about problems, not features. You’ll own something, ship it, and know whether it worked. You'll be embedded in a product team at Hudl working on software used by coaches, athletes, and parents across sports at every level, from youth leagues to professional clubs. You won't shadow a Product Manager. You'll be one.

The sports software market is competitive and growing. Hudl works across thousands of teams in dozens of sports globally, which means the product problems are genuinely complex and the users are passionate. Product Managers here get real scope early, and we take the craft seriously.

As a Product Management Intern, you’ll:

• Identify a real problem worth solving. You’ll write the user stories, and advocate for the right tradeoffs with your squad.

• Validate ideas. You’ll interview coaches, athletes, or parents and translate what you hear into clear requirements.

• Track and quantify. You’ll not just track what ships, but quantify whether it moved the needle.

• Develop strong partnerships. You’ll partner with Sales, Support, and Marketing as your team prepares for a rollout, because shipping isn't the finish line.

• Own your role. You’ll write documentation, set sprint goals, and escalate clearly when you're stuck.

Must-Haves

• A current student. You're pursuing a degree in any field, whether computer science, business, design, communications, or something adjacent. We care more about how you think than what your major says.

• Some Product Management exposure. You've encountered product management before through a class, a project, a club, or a job. You don't need to have shipped a product, but you shouldn't be starting from zero either.

• A problem-solver's instinct. You naturally ask why a problem is worth solving, not just how to solve it. You look for ways to measure whether your work actually made things better.

• Clear, confident communication. You write well, listen actively, and can advocate a point of view without needing consensus before you have one.

• Collaborative by default. You make the teams and projects you're part of better. You bring ideas, stay open to others', and don't wait to be told what to do.

• Comfort with ambiguity. Product work rarely comes with complete information. You're able to make a call, explain your reasoning, and update when you learn something new.

Nice-to-Haves

• A builder. You've created something: a software project, a small business, a community, a product prototype. You know what it feels like to take something from idea to existence, and you're familiar with the tradeoffs that come with it.

• Prior Product Management experience. You've done a product internship, worked in a product-adjacent role, or gone deep enough in a personal project to understand the basics of discovery, prioritization, and delivery.

• Competitive curiosity. You pay attention to what alternatives exist in spaces you care about. You notice what other products do well, where they fall short, and what that might mean for how you'd approach the same problem.

• Influence without authority. You've found ways to move a team or a decision in a direction you believed in, without being the person in charge.
